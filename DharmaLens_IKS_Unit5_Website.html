About this prototype

Purpose and intended users

DharmaLens turns the ethics content of IKS Unit 5 into something you can practise. Instead of only reading about Ahimsa, Satya and Dharma, users apply them to realistic AI situations and see why a choice is stronger or weaker.

Who it is for: BCA/B.Sc (IT) students and other young technology learners who will build or use AI systems.

How to use it: read the principles, work through the six scenarios in the Decision Lab, then use the audit checklist as a quick pre-launch review.

The ethical framework

Nine principles. Real decisions.

These concepts are drawn from the Unit 5 notes and applied here as a practical lens for evaluating AI systems.

🕊️
AHIMSA

Non-harm

Avoid causing harm through actions, words or systems. For AI, consider privacy violations, harmful outputs and unfair treatment.

🔎
SATYA

Truthfulness

Be honest and transparent. Explain when AI is involved, avoid misleading claims, and make decisions open to scrutiny.

🧭
DHARMA

Duty and responsibility

Developers and organizations remain responsible for systems they build. AI should support human decisions, not erase accountability.

🌍
LOKA-SANGRAHA

Collective welfare

Consider society-wide effects. A useful system should not create avoidable inequality or concentrate benefits while spreading harm.

🤝
INFORMED CONSENT

Respect user choice

People should know how their data is collected and used, and have meaningful choices wherever possible.

🛡️
HUMAN OVERSIGHT

Keep humans accountable

High-impact decisions need review, correction and appeal paths. Automation should not become an excuse to avoid responsibility.

🌈
SARVA DHARMA SAMABHAVA

Inclusivity and harmony

Equal respect for all beliefs and traditions. In technology, this means systems that respect cultural and language diversity and stay accessible to everyone.

📚
VIDYA

Knowledge as responsibility

Knowledge is treated as sacred, tied to moral development and understanding one's role. Building AI is a use of knowledge that carries ethical duty.

🏛️
GOVERNANCE

Balance innovation and protection

Regulation and company self-regulation both matter. Ethical guidelines, inspired by Ahimsa and Dharma, keep profit from coming at the cost of society.

Sources and project notes

The core ethical concepts and their technology applications below are based on the uploaded course material. The scenarios and interface are original educational examples created for this prototype.

Primary source

  • IKS Unit 1 to 5 merged notes, Unit 5: “Exploration of Ethical Frameworks in Indian Philosophy: Ahimsa, Satya, Dharma”; cultural values and societal norms; ethical considerations in AI, data privacy and technology governance.
  • IKS Innovative Project Assignment Brief, pages 1–2: Requirements for the App or Website Prototype format (Unit 5, Ethics & AI).
  • Course concepts represented: Ahimsa (non-harm), Satya (truthfulness), Dharma (duty), Loka-Sangraha (collective welfare), Sarva Dharma Samabhava (inclusivity), respect for knowledge (Vidya), informed consent, data security, technology governance, fairness and human responsibility.

No external factual claims are required for the scenarios. This website demonstrates ethical reasoning and does not claim that a particular system is legally compliant.
